# Evidence Policy

This document outlines the standards used for verifying claims and constructing "Evidence-Based Revised Responses" within this portfolio.

## 1. Source Hierarchy
When verifying an AI's claims, sources are prioritized in the following order:
1.  **Peer-Reviewed Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses:** Highest priority for established medical and scientific consensus.
2.  **Official Health and Regulatory Organizations:** (e.g., WHO, CDC, FDA, EMA) for clinical guidelines and safety information.
3.  **Peer-Reviewed Primary Research Articles:** For specific mechanistic details, recent discoveries, or domain-specific data not yet covered in reviews.
4.  **Authoritative Academic Textbooks:** For foundational scientific principles.

## 2. Preferred vs. Unacceptable Evidence

**Preferred Evidence:**
*   Articles with verifiable DOIs.
*   Information hosted on `.gov`, `.edu`, or official organizational domains.
*   Research published in reputable, high-impact journals.

**Unacceptable Evidence:**
*   Low-quality health blogs or content mills.
*   Unsupported commercial sources (e.g., a pharmaceutical company's unverified marketing materials, SEO-driven agricultural supply sites).
*   Pre-prints (unless explicitly noted as unverified and necessary for cutting-edge context).
*   AI-generated summaries (e.g., using ChatGPT to verify a fact).

## 3. Handling Conflicting Sources and Scientific Uncertainty
Science is rarely absolute. When evaluating claims where the literature is debated or multifactorial:
*   **Avoid oversimplification:** Do not replace an AI's overly simplistic answer with a different oversimplified answer. 
*   **Use cautious wording:** Utilize terms such as "primarily," "evidence indicates," "may contribute," and "the mechanism is multifactorial."
*   **Acknowledge the debate:** If multiple viable mechanisms exist, the revised response must reflect that complexity rather than declaring one "true" mechanism.

## 4. Handling Outdated Evidence
*   Evaluations are based on the scientific consensus available at the time of the review. 
*   If an AI model's training data cutoff caused an error (e.g., referencing an old guideline), it is marked as a factual error, but the limitation regarding the model's knowledge cutoff should be noted in the evaluation metadata.

## 5. Preventing Fabrication
*   Every citation provided in a revised response must be manually verified. 
*   Do not cite a source solely because the title appears relevant; the content of the source must directly support the specific correction being made.
